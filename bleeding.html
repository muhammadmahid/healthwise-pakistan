Severe Bleeding

Severe external bleeding can be life-threatening. Knowing the basic first-aid response can help while professional medical help is being obtained.

🚨 If the bleeding is severe or life-threatening:

Get emergency medical help immediately and begin appropriate first aid while help is being obtained.

How can you recognize severe bleeding?

Severe bleeding may involve blood flowing continuously or spurting, a large amount of blood loss, or signs that the person is becoming seriously unwell.

Treat suspected life-threatening bleeding as an emergency.

What should you do?

  1. Make sure the scene is safe. Avoid putting yourself in danger.
  2. Protect yourself from blood when possible. Use gloves or another barrier if available.
  3. Get emergency medical help. Severe bleeding requires urgent professional care.
  4. Apply firm, direct pressure to the wound. Use a dressing or suitable clean material and press directly over the source of bleeding.
  5. Keep applying pressure. Continue until the bleeding stops, another person takes over, or further appropriate care is provided.
Key point: Direct pressure is the primary first-aid action for life-threatening external bleeding.

What should you NOT do?

  • Do not put ice directly on the wound to try to stop life-threatening bleeding.
  • Do not rely on pressure points instead of direct pressure on the wound.
  • Do not put yourself in danger while trying to help.

What about a tourniquet?

For severe, life-threatening bleeding from an arm or leg, a tourniquet may be appropriate when direct pressure is ineffective or cannot be performed.

Manufactured tourniquets are preferred. If a tourniquet is used, emergency medical care is still required.

Important: Tourniquet use is a serious first-aid intervention. HealthWise is an educational resource, not a substitute for practical first-aid training.

Until professional help arrives

  • Stay with the injured person when it is safe to do so.
  • Continue monitoring their condition.
  • Try to keep them from becoming too cold or overheated.
  • Follow instructions from emergency responders.
